# HUMANIZE GUIDE — Removing AI Tells From "Airbnb Anywhere"

**STANDING RULE (author-mandated, applies to EVERY future edit of this book, by anyone):** any new or revised sentence must pass this guide before it lands. No new em-dashes. No "it's not just X, it's Y." No trailing appositive fragments ("— the whole stack, with the exact numbers"). No parallel fragment ladders as lists. Read every new sentence in Jeff's spoken voice before saving. When an edit is done, re-run the tell counts on the touched file and confirm nothing regressed.

Goal: the book must read like Jeff Chheuy — a pharmacist-turned-investor who talks numbers straight and has actually stood in these houses — not like a language model. The audience is W2 professionals (nurses, pharmacists, engineers, managers) with savings, no real-estate experience, and a low tolerance for guru-speak. They smell hype instantly. Concrete beats clever, always.

## The AI-tell catalog (what readers flag online) and what to do

### 1. Punctuation and construction tells
- **Em-dash overuse ("the ChatGPT dash").** Max ~1 em-dash per 2–3 paragraphs. Replace with periods, commas, or parentheses. Never two em-dashes in one sentence unless it's a true parenthetical that earns it.
- **"It's not just X — it's Y." / "This isn't X. It's Y."** The single most-flagged construction. Allow at most ONE per chapter, and only where the contrast is the actual point. Rewrite the rest as direct statements.
- **"Not X. Not Y. Not Z. Just W."** fragment ladders. Cap at one per chapter.
- **Rule-of-three triads** ("Identity. Leverage. Freedom." / "Real markets. Real numbers. Real results."). AI defaults to threes constantly. Keep a triad only when Jeff's original slides used it; elsewhere vary to two items, four items, or a plain sentence.
- **Anaphora spam** ("You don't need A. You don't need B. You don't need C."). One anaphora run per chapter max; vary the others.
- **Colon-title constructions** in headers ("X: Why Y Matters"). Prefer plain, specific headers.
- **Perfectly parallel bullet lists** where every bullet starts with the same part of speech and has the same length. Roughen them: different lengths, occasional aside.

### 2. Vocabulary tells (replace on sight unless in a direct quote or a framework's established name)
delve, navigate (metaphorical), landscape (metaphorical), tapestry, testament, beacon, elevate, unlock, unleash, supercharge, game-changer, seamless/seamlessly, robust, crucial, pivotal, foster, harness, realm, embark, journey (metaphorical), empower, transformative, revolutionize, dive deep/deep dive, at its core, in essence, ultimately, moreover, furthermore, additionally (sentence-initial), "the world of", "when it comes to", "in today's [fast-paced] world", "look no further", "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "needless to say", "arguably", "truly", "incredibly", "remarkably", "effortlessly", "a wealth of", "a myriad of", "plethora".
Exception: "leverage" is a genuine finance term in this book (leverage ratio, leveraged capital) — keep the NOUN in financial contexts; kill the VERB in generic contexts ("leverage your network" → "use your network").

### 3. Rhythm and structure tells
- **Uniform paragraph length.** AI writes 2–3 sentence paragraphs forever. Vary deliberately: an occasional one-word or one-line paragraph, an occasional 5–6 sentence story paragraph. The pattern must not be a pattern.
- **Short. Punchy. Fragments. Everywhere.** Keep Jeff's aphorisms ("Amateurs chase revenue. Pros guard the payment." "Booked is not banked.") but they lose power if every third paragraph ends in one. Space them out; one strong aphorism per section beats four weak ones.
- **The setup-question tic** ("So what does this mean for you? Simple."). Cut the rhetorical question or make it a real one Jeff answers with substance.
- **"Here's the truth/kicker/thing/deal:"** Max once per chapter, total across all variants.
- **Every section ending in a mic-drop line.** Let some sections just end.

### 4. Content tells
- **Symmetric hedging** ("While X can be powerful, it's important to consider Y"). Jeff has opinions. State them.
- **Empty summarizing** ("In short, choosing the right market matters."). Delete or replace with a specific consequence ("Get this wrong and the hot tub can't save you.").
- **Generic examples** ("imagine a property in a small town"). This book has real properties with real numbers — always prefer them.
- **Over-signposting** ("In this chapter, we will explore..."). One promise sentence max, phrased like a person ("By the end of this chapter you'll know exactly what I'd pay for that cabin.").
- **"Whether you're a first-timer or a seasoned investor..."** Cut entirely.

### 4b. Stylometric tells (2026 research addendum — these survive vocabulary fixes)
- **Sentence-length clustering.** Three or more consecutive sentences of 17–23 words is the flag; it's what detector "burstiness" metrics measure. Slam a short sentence against a long one regularly.
- **Sentence-starter monotony.** More than half the sentences in a paragraph opening with "The," "This," "It," or "In." Vary the openings.
- **Trailing present-participle clauses.** LLMs use them at 2–5x the human rate ("...ensuring accountability," "...making it easier to scale"). End the sentence instead; start a new one.
- **Vague-authority attribution.** "Studies show," "experts say," "data suggests" with no named source. This book cites real sources or speaks from Jeff's own operating experience; nothing in between.
- **Importance inflation.** Things "playing a vital role," "underscoring the importance of." Cut on sight.
- **Identical chapter template.** The most-cited flaw in AI nonfiction: every chapter shaped definition → three examples → summary. Our chapters must vary how they move (story-led, math-led, checklist-led).
- **Uniform emotional temperature.** AI narrative stays placid and resolves every beat warmly. Keep the real costs on the page (the botched renovation, the fire, the missed deal); don't sand tension into a tidy bow.
- **Negative-parallelism padding.** Five parallel restatements of one idea posing as a list. One idea, said once, well.
- **Identically-shaped "card" boxes.** Framework boxes (What/Why/When/Lesson) whose fields are all clipped fragment strings of the same length read generated. Vary field lengths; write "Why it works" as one spoken sentence with a mechanism, not a slogan.

### 5. What makes it feel HUMAN (add, don't just subtract)
- **Specificity of memory**: dates, road names, dollar figures, what the text message actually said, the smell of a just-cleaned cabin, the $11 propane surcharge. Where the draft has a generic beat, sharpen with a detail that already exists in the book (never invent new facts/numbers/scenes).
- **Asides and admissions**: "I know how this sounds." "My banker thought I was nuts." "I still check the app more than I should." Small, honest, imperfect. (Only where consistent with known facts; don't fabricate events.)
- **Direct address that sounds spoken**: read sentences aloud in your head; if a tired pharmacist wouldn't say it to a friend over coffee, rewrite it until they would.
- **Occasional informality**: sentences starting with And/But/Because, a "look," a "fine," contractions everywhere.
- **Numbers written like an operator talks**: "$1,000 a month, principal only" not "a favorable principal-only financing arrangement."

## Hard constraints
- Do NOT change any facts, numbers, names, or claims. Do NOT add new anecdotes, events, or sensory details that assert new facts (an aside like "I know how this sounds" is fine; "I remember the rain that day" is NOT unless the source says so).
- Do NOT touch heading structure, chapter numbers, part openers' RURAL definitions, or the existence/position of `### Chapter Summary` sections (you may reword bullets to kill tells).
- Keep the book's established framework names exactly as they are (The Candy Bar Method, The 30% Floor, etc.).
- Keep the James Clear attribution in Part 1 and the callback in Part 2; add no other book/author mentions.
- Keep total word count within ~3% of the file's current count. This is a rewrite of texture, not length.
- Banned names stay banned: Hogan, Astrid, Chris, Florence.
- Chapter Summaries: bullets should be memorable but not machine-parallel; vary their shapes too.
