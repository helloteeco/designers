# The Six-Week Launch Checklist

**From contract to first guest, week by week.**
Companion to Chapter 13 of *Vacation Rentals That Pay You*. Print it, tape it to the wall, check the boxes.

---

## The Timing Rules (read these first)

- **The target: keys in hand to listing live in six weeks.** Block six weeks on your calendar post-close, **plus a buffer**. If something slips a week, you don't miss your launch window.
- **The clock starts before closing.** Escrow is prep time, not waiting time. Everything in Phase 0 below happens while you're under contract.
- **Best case:** a reno-light home can compress to about **one week** — keys, install, staging, photos.
- **Worst case:** a botched fixer can stretch a few-week setup into **four months** and eat a third of your year. This is why structural changes are never worth the timeline delays. Skip them.
- **Photos are the gate.** The shoot happens in week 3 or 4, not week 6. Everything downstream waits on it, so schedule the photographer before you think you're ready.
- **Speed is the point.** Every empty night is your biggest cost. Perception beats perfection.

---

## Phase 0 — Under Contract (Before You Close)

- [ ] **Line up contractor bids.** Paint, flooring, handyman work — bids in hand so work begins Day 1 after closing.
- [ ] **Complete your inspections** inside the contingency period.
- [ ] **Get a bindable STR insurance quote before your inspection contingency expires.** A quote you could accept on the spot, not a website estimate. Homeowners policies void the moment you host; a rural cabin with a hot tub often quotes $2,500–$6,000 a year. A high premium eats the deal's cushion — know the number before you're locked in.
- [ ] **Pre-schedule utilities, internet, and insurance.** Set start dates for electric, water, WiFi, and the STR policy to activate right after closing.
- [ ] **Create a project calendar.** Block the first six weeks post-close plus a buffer. Plan the order: light reno → furniture install → photos → listing → launch.
- [ ] **Prep your furniture financing.** For many hosts that's a 0% intro-APR card — **but only if you'll clear it inside the intro window. A bridge, not a balance.** Chapter 7's rules apply.
- [ ] **Load the furniture carts — but do not order anything until you own the home.** Deals fall through.

## Closing Day

- [ ] **Hit "order" on the major furniture pieces** the moment you close. Deliver to the property or a storage unit.
- [ ] **Confirm utilities, internet, and STR insurance are active.**

---

## Weeks 1–2 — Contractor Work and Light Reno

- [ ] Contractor work and inspections underway from Day 1.
- [ ] Light reno only: **paint and fixtures.** Splurge where it shows on camera — beds, lighting, paint, art and décor. Skip full remodels, luxury flooring, high-end appliances, and anything structural.
- [ ] Pre-order any remaining major furniture (mattresses $300–$500 per bed; linens and pillows $200–$300 per bed — comfort drives reviews).
- [ ] Quick wins as budget allows: swapped light fixtures, peel-and-stick backsplash, oversized art or mirrors, front-door and outdoor-lighting refresh, fire pit.
- [ ] Start lining up your boots-on-the-ground team: **your first cleaner is your most important hire — treat them like a partner.** Also: a handyman, lawn or snow care.

## Weeks 2–3 — Furniture Install and Styling

- [ ] Furniture delivery and installation.
- [ ] Interior styling and décor. Design for photos first, comfort second. Maximize sleepers per room (bunks, trundles, pull-out sofas) — sleeping capacity is revenue.
- [ ] **Hire the photographer if you haven't.** The shoot is the gate for everything downstream.
- [ ] Create a detailed inventory checklist.
- [ ] If you hired design help: approve mood boards and picks within 24 hours. You are the bottleneck if you let yourself be.

## Weeks 3–4 — Photography and Systems

- [ ] **Professional photography shoot.** No other line item earns back more per dollar.
- [ ] Set up your systems:
  - [ ] Messaging automation (Hospitable)
  - [ ] Pricing tool (PriceLabs)
  - [ ] Security hardware: smart lock with unique per-guest codes, exterior camera only — indoor cameras never
- [ ] **Create the listing on Airbnb.**

## Weeks 4–5 — Listing Build and Pricing Ladder

- [ ] Fine-tune the listing: hero photos, title, description.
  - [ ] Lead with what's unique and searchable ("Hot Tub | Fire Pit | Minutes to Hiking"), not bed counts.
  - [ ] Sell the dream in the first five lines; save beds and kitchen features for later.
  - [ ] 30–50 high-quality photos. If you paid thousands for it, it's in the first five photos and the title.
- [ ] Set launch pricing and calendar rules:
  - [ ] Launch price **15–20% below comps** (the Pricing Ladder assumes the full 20%)
  - [ ] 3-night minimum
  - [ ] Instant Book on from day one
  - [ ] Plan the ladder now: 20% under at launch → 10% under in weeks 3–4 → market rate by month 2 → 5–10% over by months 3–6 once your rating is 4.8+
- [ ] **Pre-list to build early algorithm interest.** Listing live, calendar closed; send the link to friends and followers to wishlist it.
- [ ] List on both Airbnb and Vrbo.
- [ ] Confirm cleaner, handyman, and a short local vendor list (plumber, electrician, HVAC) are ready.

## Week 6 — Launch

- [ ] **Go live.** Open the calendar and start accepting bookings.
- [ ] Monitor messages closely — respond within 15 minutes during the first two weeks; under an hour is a ranking factor.
- [ ] Prepare for first guests: ultra-clear instructions, thoughtful touches (firewood, local treats, a handwritten note).

## After Go-Live — The First Month

- [ ] Answer every message fast, personally at first; automate warm replies once patterns emerge.
- [ ] Climb the Pricing Ladder on schedule; update base price and rules every 2–4 weeks, watch trends weekly.
- [ ] **Collect your first 3–5 reviews.** They make or break early ranking. Fix problems fast, pair the fix with a small gesture, and send a short follow-up after checkout asking for the review.
- [ ] Set up bookkeeping from day one: revenue, operating expenses, cap-ex, loan payments, and a receipt system. Clean books equal clean tax write-offs.

---

That first booking notification is execution, not luck. None of it reaches the bank until you launch.
