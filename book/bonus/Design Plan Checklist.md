# Design Plan Checklist

Design isn't decoration. It's your booking engine, and there's no neutral design; it's either working for you or against you. Fill this in before you buy a single piece of furniture. Work the four sections in order: guest profile, function, durability, aesthetics. Before every purchase, ask one question: "Does this look good in a photo?" Design lives on the screen, not in the room.

## 1. Guest Profile

Your anchor already picked your guest. A national park pulls families and adventure seekers, a hospital or a base pulls work travelers, a scenic getaway town pulls couples. You don't have to appeal to everyone. You have to nail it for one.

- [ ] **My anchor:** ______________________ **My guest avatar:** ______________________
- [ ] **Design list for that avatar checked:**
  - **Families:** queen bunks, trundle beds, toy baskets, board games, kid-friendly dishware, durable rugs, a table large enough for everyone at once
  - **Couples:** romantic lighting, plush bedding, robes, a fire pit, cozy nooks built for two
  - **Work travelers:** a desk nook with an ergonomic chair (or a standing desk), fast WiFi, lighting that doesn't ruin video calls
  - **Adventure seekers:** gear hooks, boot trays, lockable storage, an outdoor shower, a welcome book that points them to the good stuff
- [ ] **Comp audit done before spending a dollar.** Top 3–5 comps: screenshot 10–15 photos each, read the reviews for what guests praise and complain about, check pricing and occupancy, and name the gap you'll fill. Gap: ______________________

## 2. Function

You're not decorating a house. You're building a machine that sleeps people for money.

- [ ] **Capacity plan:** ________ sqft ÷ 100 = ______ target sleepers. Queen-over-queen bunks, trundles, and pull-out sofas close the gap (buy a good pull-out; a bad one generates bad reviews).
- [ ] **Seating and dining match the sleeper count.** [MAX GUESTS] dining chairs, [MAX GUESTS] place settings, [MAX GUESTS] mugs, and living-room seating where the whole group can land at once. You can't sleep twelve and seat four.
- [ ] **Amenities picked by the Candy Bar Method.** Validate every amenity with your market's data (Rabbu or similar) before you buy it. Roughly $10K invested strategically in amenities has commonly generated $1K–$5K per month in additional revenue across our homes. The ranked list: hot tub (install $3K–$8K, commonly adds $50–$100 per night in my markets), fire pit ($200 plus chairs), game room ($2K–$5K, has lifted occupancy 10–20% in our leisure-market homes), quality linens and pillows ($200–$300 per bed, non-negotiable), outdoor shower ($1K–$3K in warm or adventure markets).
- [ ] **Budget tier chosen:**

| Tier | Cost | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| DIY | $10–$20 per square foot, plus your labor | Furnishings and décor only; you do everything |
| Design-only plan | $3K–$5K | A plan and a shopping list; no sourcing, no install, no staging |
| Hybrid | $3K–$5K plan + a VA for logistics | Most of the polish for a fraction of the fee |
| Full-service | $8K+ in design fees; $20K–$40K+ all-in with sourcing, install, and labor | Market research, avatar work, trade discounts, install crews, a photo-ready home |

- [ ] **Design ROI run with your market's numbers, not the book's.** Payback = Design Cost ÷ (New Revenue − Old Revenue). Reference math: $30K of design lifting ADR $220 to $260 and occupancy 55% to 70% adds $22,265 a year and pays back in about 16 months. If payback is under 18 months, spend the money.

## 3. Durability

Pretty design is useless if it falls apart after three groups of guests. The Hotel Standard Rule: if Hilton uses it, you should too.

- [ ] **Sofas:** performance fabric or leather. No light fabrics or delicate materials.
- [ ] **Rugs:** patterned, washable, or low-pile. They hide dirt and clean easily.
- [ ] **Beds:** solid wood frames or platform beds with center support. Cheap metal frames squeak and wobble.
- [ ] **Tables:** solid wood. Veneer chips fast.
- [ ] **Hardware:** matte black, bronze, or brushed nickel. Chrome shows every smudge.
- [ ] **Linens:** white towels and sheets only. Easy to bleach, consistent everywhere.

## 4. Aesthetics

Four decisions. Make them once, write them down, and every purchase afterward either fits the plan or goes back in the cart.

- [ ] **1. Style lane (pick one, never mix):** ☐ Modern Cabin ☐ Coastal Chic ☐ Midcentury Modern ☐ Scandi Minimal ☐ Other: ____________
- [ ] **2. Color palette, 3–4 tones max:** ______________________________
- [ ] **3. Repeated elements** (same metals, woods, textures in every room): ______________________________
- [ ] **4. One signature moment** guests photograph and share (mural wall, statement headboard, neon sign): ______________________________

Then plan the rooms:

- [ ] **Living:** sofa plus accent chairs (no two loveseats), rug large enough that all front furniture legs sit on it, styled coffee table, one or two oversized art pieces
- [ ] **Bedrooms:** real headboard, minimum 4 pillows per bed with a throw and blanket, matching nightstands and identical lamps, mirror or bench if space allows
- [ ] **Dining:** full-size table (extendable if possible), seating for max occupancy always, centerpiece, art or mirror to bounce light
- [ ] **Kitchen:** spotless counters with one or two styled touches (coffee station, plant, cookbook), essentials guest-ready
- [ ] **Bathrooms:** white towel sets, staged tray with soaps, spa touches (rolled towels, bath salts, candles)
- [ ] **Outdoors:** fire pit staged with at least 4 chairs, hot tub with jets on and cover visible, string lights at dusk
- [ ] **Lighting, three layers everywhere:** ambient, task, accent. Warm white (2700–3000K) in bedrooms and living areas, neutral white (3500K) in kitchens and baths, warm string lights and lanterns outside.
- [ ] **3–5 Instagram moments planned:** the welcome, the view or unique spot, the gathering spot, the bedroom escape, the detail
- [ ] **20% local flavor, 80% timeless design.** Local materials and one or two pieces from local makers, not bear wallpaper in every room.
