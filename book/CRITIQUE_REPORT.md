## What the veteran attacked, and how the book now answers

1. **"The return bands are 2021 fantasy."** → Tier bands are now labeled "(top-quartile operators, 2021–24)" with today's anchor: a well-executed first deal more often pencils to 15–25% at current rates — and that still beats nearly everything available to a beginner.
2. **"The salary comparison is a strawman."** → Chapter 1 now credits the index-fund saver honestly and states that $2,000/month net is the earned result of doing everything in the book right, not the participation prize.
3. **"Your insurance numbers are from a dead market."** → $2,500–$6,000/year for rural cabins with hot tubs and wood stoves; homeowners policies void when you host; platform damage programs are not insurance; get a bindable quote before your inspection contingency expires. In both due diligence (Ch 6) and the expense tables (Ch 8).
4. **"Property tax reassessment and lodging taxes — missing."** → Now in Hidden Costs: model taxes at YOUR purchase price, ask about commercial reclassification, call the county about lodging tax before you list.
5. **"Regulation risk is scored, not planned for."** → Covered via the LTR-fallback rule (below) plus written zoning confirmation guidance.
6. **"The 'capped downside' claim is rhetoric."** → The worst case is now quantified (down payment + setup capital + months of negative carry — bounded, real money) and the insurance rule is printed: before you buy, check that market long-term rent covers ~80% of your payment.
7. **"Your stress test drops occupancy but not rate."** → Now a 25–30% *revenue* haircut, with the nuclear scenario: does it survive at long-term market rent?
8. **"The whole plan assumes the month-12 refi works."** → The refi-doesn't-come branch is in Chapter 17: 70–75% LTV with seasoning is typical, and if the appraisal or rate doesn't cooperate you save cash flow and buy a year later. "The plan slows; it doesn't break. Never make deal two dependent on deal one's appraisal."
9. **"Five case studies, zero losses — survivorship museum."** → DELIVERED: the student who overspent and had everything go wrong is now in Chapter 8, right after the stress test — still cash flows ~$1,000/month, kept the write-offs, home appreciated. "A worst case you can live with." 
10. **"Is the window already shut?"** → Answered head-on in Chapter 5: the easy era is over, professionals run the same screens, the method works because most buyers still skip the order of operations — and the extra margin to demand now: trailing-twelve comps, 10–15% projection haircut.
11. **"Three hours a week is the median sold as the product."** → It's now explicitly the median week, with the honest annual profile (a handful of ugly weeks; year one is front-loaded) framed as the case FOR the systems.
12. **"Your 90-day occupancy expectations contradict your own underwriting."** → Fixed to 65–75%, with the launch-season caveat and "stabilized means 55–70% over a full year."
13. **"$1,000 doesn't buy a water heater."** → The capex sinking fund is in: 1–2% of home value per year, minimum $10K liquid per property, sized by the inspection.
14. **"The Stack Strategy page will hurt someone."** → Rebuilt: the example buyer now starts with $45K and closes with six months of PITI plus cushion. "Never close so thin that one slow season forces bad decisions."
15. **"The card program understates correlated failure."** → The cap rule (never float more than six months of conservative cash flow can retire) plus the warning that maxed cards sabotage the refi application the plan depends on.
16. **"The second-home rider fine print is missing."** → Two sentences: disclose rental intent, read the rider, and if it conflicts with your plan (including start-with-a-PM), price the DSCR loan.
17. **"Your DSCR example wouldn't qualify at most lenders."** → The one question to ask is in, plus the rural denial math — and Diane and Jon's loan is now identified as a typical 15%-down DSCR program that underwrites STR rent, closing the loop.
18. **"Ryu's 0% note is a unicorn presented as a template."** → Labeled a top-1% outcome; the typical carry (10% down, 6–7%, 5-year balloon) is shown; "the balloon is your refinance risk wearing a different hat."
19. **"Platform dependence gets one paragraph at Level 5."** → Suspension/payout-freeze/policy-change risk now in the ops chapter; second platform early; reserves as platform insurance.
20. **"Amenity ROI is anecdote dressed as arithmetic."** → Every amenity/design number is now hedged as "what strong design commonly moves in my markets," with the printed rule: buy the amenity you can see in your own market's data, not the one in this book.
21. **"The risk-adjusted table is the most guru artifact in the book."** → Dead. Replaced with an honest qualitative trade-off table and the sentence: rural STRs win on return and control; they lose on liquidity and labor; that's the trade.
22. **"You remember gross to the penny and go foggy on every net."** → DELIVERED: "The Page I'd Show a Skeptic" is now in Chapter 8 — Gatehouse Getaway's real 2024 statement, seven months live, thirteen expense lines (including an honest ~$300/month electric estimate) down to $22,502 net, with estimate notes, ~34% cash-on-cash on ~$66K in, and the $1,125 interest-only note now corrected throughout the book (the old ~$1,250 was from memory).
23. **Liability (bonus):** → $1–2M umbrella + "the LLC question is about liability isolation at least as much as taxes; ask the attorney, not just the CPA."
24. **Exit reality (bonus):** → Exits anchored to appraised value and 6–12 month timelines; the 8–12x turnkey exit labeled the exception; "surprisingly liquid" removed; the bunk-room-narrows-the-buyer-pool caveat added.
