# book-rules.md — The Bestseller Standard for *Vacation Rentals That Pay You*

This file is the source of truth for HOW the book should read. Any revision pass must
measure a chapter against these rules. If a sentence doesn't earn its place under these
rules, cut it.

---

## 1. The one-sentence job of the book
Teach a high-income, no-experience professional how to buy and run a rural short-term
rental that actually pays — using receipts, not hype. Every chapter moves the reader one
concrete step closer to owning one.

---

## 2. The chapter spine (the "Atomic Habits" structure)
Every chapter follows this seven-beat spine. Not every beat is equal length, but every
beat is present and in this order:

1. **Cold open (a scene, not a throat-clear).** Start inside a specific moment, number,
   or story. Never "In this chapter, we'll cover…". Drop the reader into something real.
2. **The tension.** Name the problem this chapter resolves and why it costs the reader
   money, time, or freedom if ignored. Raise the stakes.
3. **The core principle — named.** ONE idea per chapter. Give it a sticky, repeatable
   name (the way *Atomic Habits* names "the 1% rule"). If the reader remembers one thing,
   this is it.
4. **The receipts.** Prove it. Real numbers, real addresses, real occupancy, real dollars
   — from Jeff's own portfolio or named case studies. Anti-guru: show receipts or cut the
   claim.
5. **The mechanism.** The tactical how-to. Step-by-step, do-this-not-that, specific tools
   and thresholds. This is where the reader learns to actually execute.
6. **The failure modes.** What goes wrong, who this is NOT for, the mistake most people
   make. Honesty here builds more trust than any promise.
7. **Land it + one action.** A tight summary (a short checklist is ideal) and ONE concrete
   next step the reader takes before the next chapter. End with momentum pulling forward.

---

## 3. Voice rules (non-negotiable)
- **Short sentences. One idea each. Vary the rhythm.** A punchy three-word line after a
  long one. Read it aloud — if you run out of breath, cut it.
- **Concrete beats abstract every time.** "$2,140/month in Joshua Tree" not "strong
  returns." Numbers, dates, addresses, dollar figures.
- **Keep Jeff human.** Preserve the real stories and the imperfect edges: the immigrant
  scarcity mindset turned into capital efficiency, the healthcare career, the tension of
  leaving a stable salary, the first messy deal. Never sand these into generic motivation.
- **Second person, direct.** Talk to "you." Give orders where earned.
- **Anti-guru at all times.** No hype, no "passive income while you sleep," no fake
  scarcity. If it can't be backed with a receipt or a mechanism, it doesn't ship.
- **Kill the filler.** Ban: "in today's fast-paced world," "the truth is," "at the end of
  the day," "unlock," "leverage" (as a verb), "game-changer," any sentence that could
  appear in any book on any topic.

---

## 4. Global "bestseller DNA" checks (whole-book level)
- **One big idea per chapter, reinforced.** The reader should be able to state each
  chapter's principle in a sentence.
- **Stories carry the teaching.** The principle rides on a scene; it is not announced
  and defended like a lecture.
- **Ruthless clarity.** A smart friend with zero real-estate background must follow every
  page without re-reading.
- **Actionable after every chapter.** The reader can DO something before turning the page.
- **The 70% rule, modeled not preached.** The book should push the reader toward action
  with enough clarity, not toward more research. It practices what it teaches.

---

## 5. Acceptance test for any revised chapter
A chapter passes only if ALL are true:
- [ ] Opens with a scene/number/story, not a summary.
- [ ] Has exactly one named core principle.
- [ ] Every claim has a receipt or a mechanism behind it.
- [ ] Contains a concrete step-by-step the reader can execute.
- [ ] Names at least one failure mode or "not for you if…".
- [ ] Ends with a summary/checklist + one specific action.
- [ ] Jeff's real voice and stories are intact and sharper, not generic.
- [ ] No banned filler phrases remain.
